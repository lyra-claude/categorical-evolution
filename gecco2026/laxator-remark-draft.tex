\begin{remark}[The laxator and the spectral bridge]
\label{rem:laxator}
The Dichotomy admits a precise categorical formulation.
Let $\mathcal{I}_G$ denote the island functor for migration graph~$G$,
lifting a single-population strategy $\sigma : P \to M\,P$ to the
$n$-island system $\mathcal{I}_G(\sigma) : P^n \to M\,P^n$.
The \emph{laxator} is the natural transformation
\[
  \varphi_G \;:\;
  \mathcal{I}_G(\sigma_1) \circ \mathcal{I}_G(\sigma_2)
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \mathcal{I}_G(\sigma_1 \circ \sigma_2),
\]
measuring the discrepancy between composing lifted strategies and
lifting a composed strategy.  When $G$ has no edges,
$\varphi_G = \mathrm{id}$ and composition is strict; any connected
$G$ forces $\varphi_G \neq \mathrm{id}$.

The magnitude of $\varphi_G$ is governed by the algebraic
connectivity $\lambda_2(G)$, the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of the
graph Laplacian.  For the ring $C_n$,
$\lambda_2 = 2(1 - \cos(2\pi/n)) \approx 4\pi^2/n^2$; for the star
$K_{1,n-1}$, $\lambda_2 = 1$; for the complete graph $K_n$,
$\lambda_2 = n$.  Larger $\lambda_2$ means faster mixing, stronger
migration coupling, and a larger laxator---hence greater diversity
erosion.  The predicted ordering (ring most diverse, FC least) matches
all five domains in Section~4.2.

For fixed topologies, composition factors cleanly over time:
$\lambda_2$ of the cumulative adjacency equals $T \cdot \lambda_2$
of a single snapshot.  For the time-varying random topology
(resampled each migration event), this factoring fails: the
time-averaged effective $\lambda_2 = 39.74$, a $110\times$ inflation
over the snapshot mean of~$0.36$.  This inflation is the laxator's
numerical signature---the quantitative gap between strict and lax
composition.

The explicit construction of $\varphi_G$ and verification of its
coherence conditions remain open; the present paper identifies the
laxator as the governing quantity and confirms its spectral proxy
empirically.
\end{remark}
